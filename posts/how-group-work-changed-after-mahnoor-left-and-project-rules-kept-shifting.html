How Group Work Changed After Mahnoor Left and Project Rules Kept Shifting

People leaving and rules changing affected my class life more than I expected.

A class does not run only on announcements. It runs on responsible people. I learned that more clearly when one of them left.

What changed when Mahnoor left

Mahnoor Fatima had become an important part of class coordination because the earlier CR and GR setup had created problems. She handled responsibility seriously, and that mattered more than people usually realize. When she transferred to Lahore, the batch felt the difference.

What changed was not only emotional atmosphere. Coordination started slipping more often. Delayed quizzes, assignments, and communication problems made it obvious that academic routine depends heavily on a few people doing their work properly.

How that overlapped with project changes

Around the same time, my own approach to project work was changing too. For DBS, I wanted to work alone at first because it felt like the first moment when I wanted to fully test myself. I even turned down some group options. But then the rules kept shifting, and pair-or-single constraints changed the practical reality of the situation.

In the end, I adapted and moved forward with Muhammad Umair. That adaptation mattered because it reminded me that serious work is not only about strong personal intent. It is also about responding intelligently to the structure around you.

What this taught me about engineering life

This experience taught me that engineering education is social as well as technical. The systems around you, the reliability of people, and the rules of the moment all shape what becomes possible. Learning how to work through those changes is part of becoming more mature in the field.

Reflection Points

  • When Mahnoor left for Lahore, the batch felt the difference in responsibility and coordination.
  • Delayed quizzes and assignments showed how dependent routine is on reliable people.
  • DBS project rules pushed me from wanting solo work to adapting with Muhammad Umair.
