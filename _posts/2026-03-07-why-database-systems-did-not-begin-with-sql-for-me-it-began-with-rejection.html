What DBS first gave me

Database Systems entered my second semester at the right time because I was ready for another subject built around logic and structure. SQL immediately felt different from what I had already done in PF, but the same part of my mind was active. Instead of building program flow, I was learning how to think about data, relationships, and order.

That is what made DBS meaningful for me from the beginning. It did not feel like empty theory. It felt like a way of organizing thought.

Why my first project idea failed

Sir initially asked us to create a website project using WordPress, XAMPP or WAMP, and MySQL. I proposed an e-commerce dropshipping idea where I would source products from platforms like Daraz and Amazon. The idea felt workable to me, but it was rejected because the project needed stronger local relevance.

That rejection could have simply discouraged me, but instead it became the real beginning of my DBS journey. Sir suggested two directions: blog writing or moving toward AI, ML, and DL. I chose AI. The moment I said yes to that shift, the course stopped feeling blocked and started feeling more aligned with me.

Why the rejection helped me

Looking back, I think the rejection helped because it forced me to say something more honest about myself. My first idea was workable, but it was not really aligned with the deeper direction I want. AI and ML are. That is why I now see the beginning of DBS not as a failed proposal, but as the moment I was pushed toward a sharper version of my own interest.
