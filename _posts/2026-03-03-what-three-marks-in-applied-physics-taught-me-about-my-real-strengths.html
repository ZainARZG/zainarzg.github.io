Where the gap showed itself

In Applied Physics theory, I had one of the clearest reminders that being hardworking is not the same thing as preparing in the right way. I performed badly in the first quiz and got only three marks. That result felt like a message that I had not understood the subject on its own terms.

I am naturally stronger in logic, mathematics, practical work, and subjects where understanding can be tested directly. Applied Physics theory depended more on a type of preparation that did not come naturally to me. That is what made it difficult.

Why PF mattered during that period

While Applied Physics was testing my weaker side, Programming Fundamentals was strengthening my better side. PF kept reminding me that I do have a natural place in engineering study, especially when logic and building are involved.

The contrast between the two courses taught me a useful truth about myself. I should not judge my entire potential based on the subject that fits me least. I should understand where my strengths are, where my weaknesses are, and how to work honestly in both spaces.

Why the final result still matters

I kept working, adjusted my approach, and eventually finished Applied Physics theory with an A- and the lab with an A. That mattered to me because it was built on adaptation, not on comfort.
