I would tell myself not to mistake difficulty for misdirection

If I could speak to the version of myself who had just reached UET, I would not try to make the journey sound easy. I would not promise a perfect routine, simple focus, or constant motivation. Instead, I would explain that the hard parts are real but not necessarily signs that I chose the wrong path. Hostel adjustment, pressure, travel, tiredness, emotional distraction, and uncertainty are all part of the experience. They do affect the work, but they do not cancel the reason I came here.

I would especially tell myself to notice Programming Fundamentals early. That subject will matter more than it first appears. It will become the first course that feels deeply personal because it matches the way I think. It will give me confidence when I need it most. I would say: do not ignore that feeling. When a subject makes you feel more like yourself, pay attention. That is not a small thing. It is a clue about the kind of work you may keep returning to in the future.

I would explain the value of structure sooner

I would also tell myself that later, Database Systems will matter in a different way. It may not arrive with the same immediate emotional impact as PF, but it will train something equally important: structure. SQL and database thinking will start teaching a more disciplined way of organizing information. I would say to my earlier self that this matters, even if it feels less exciting at first. Not every important subject arrives through instant excitement. Some become valuable slowly because they reshape how you think.

I would also be more honest about routine. I would say that talent, curiosity, and effort are all useful, but they are not protected automatically. Sleep matters. Timing matters. Discipline matters. A missed rhythm can damage good work very quickly. That lesson will come through things like almost missing a quiz, trying to recover from a weak result, or carrying academic pressure through tiredness. I would say: learn the lesson earlier if you can, because routine is not separate from success. It is one of its foundations.

I would remind myself that the story is still being built

Most of all, I would tell that earlier version of me not to judge the whole journey from any single week. Some weeks will feel strong and aligned. Others will feel messy, distracted, or heavier than expected. But the larger direction will still be forming underneath all of that. There will be a serious first project. There will be difficult travel days, unexpected responsibilities, good academic outcomes, and moments that reveal both strength and weakness more clearly. All of that belongs to the same education.

If I had to reduce the message to one line, it would be this: stay with the work long enough for it to become yours. That is what PF first taught me, what Database Systems is teaching me now in another form, and what the whole university experience keeps repeating in different ways. The goal is not to become someone who never struggles. The goal is to become someone who keeps building through struggle with more clarity each time. That is what I would tell myself at the beginning, and it is still what I most need to remember now.
