Before university, I already loved computers, but loving computers and studying Computer Engineering are not the same thing. That difference became clear to me in Programming Fundamentals. When Dr Bilal started teaching Python, I felt something settle in my mind. For the first time, a subject was not asking me to repeat information. It was asking me to think.

What I liked most was the honesty of programming. If the logic was weak, the program exposed it. If the structure was careless, the output showed it. Nothing had to be guessed emotionally. I could test, fail, debug, and improve. That made PF feel natural to me in a way many theory-heavy courses did not.

I spent many late nights in the hostel on PF labs, not always because I was stuck, but because I wanted the work to look like mine. Running code was not enough for me. I wanted to understand why it worked and whether it could be cleaner. That habit of staying with the problem until it feels right became one of the main things that shaped my first semester.

Looking back, PF was not just my first programming course. It was the first course that gave me confidence that I really belong in this field.
