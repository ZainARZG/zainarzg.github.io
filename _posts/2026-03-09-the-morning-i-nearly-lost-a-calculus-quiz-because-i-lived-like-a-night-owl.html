--- layout: post title: "The Morning I Nearly Lost a Calculus Quiz Because I Lived Like a Night Owl" date: 2025-03-09 00:00:00 +0500 summary: "One winter morning showed me how quickly a late routine can turn into an academic risk." subpoints: - "Winter smog, late waking, ironed clothes, and forgotten glasses turned one ordinary morning into a near disaster." - "Getting full marks after reaching late felt like mercy, not entitlement, and it forced me to think harder about discipline." - "At the same time, my PF routine was teaching me that logic works only when I show up for it on time." ---

I still remember that Calculus I quiz morning very clearly because almost everything went wrong in a small chain. It was winter, there was smog, and I woke up late because I assumed the teacher would probably be late as well. That assumption was my first mistake.

I pressed my clothes, then realized I had forgotten my glasses in the room and had to go back for them. By the time I reached class, I was around fifteen to twenty minutes late and the quiz had already started. In that moment I felt I had thrown away marks through routine failure, not through lack of ability.

Fortunately, because many students were affected and assignment context was involved, I still received full marks and later earned an A in Calculus I. I was grateful, but I also understood that I had been saved by circumstance, not by discipline.

That moment stayed with me because PF had already started teaching me something similar in a different language: logic does not forgive loose assumptions. If I want the result, I have to respect the process.
