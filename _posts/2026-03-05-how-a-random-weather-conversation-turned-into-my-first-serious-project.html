My weather prediction project is still one of the most personal parts of my first semester because the idea did not arrive in a very formal way. I remember discussing something connected to weather in a ChatGPT conversation, and later, when Dr Bilal talked about training a model, that earlier thought returned to me with much more force.

The result became our project, "Supervised Learning Approach for Next Day Temperature and Precipitation Forecasting in Basel." I built it with Muhammad Tahir Yaqoob. Because he was also my roommate, the collaboration felt smooth, practical, and easy to continue even after class hours.

This project mattered because it was the first time I felt AI and Machine Learning moving from interest to action. Reading about models is one thing. Building one around a real dataset, understanding the training logic, and owning the result feels very different.

The exhibition made that feeling stronger. Standing in front of teachers, students, and seniors and explaining what we had made forced me to defend the work properly. That was the moment the project stopped feeling like only a course task and started feeling like part of my identity.
