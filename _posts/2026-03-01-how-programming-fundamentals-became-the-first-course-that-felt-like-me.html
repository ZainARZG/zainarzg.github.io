Why this course mattered to me

Before university, I already loved computers, but loving computers and studying Computer Engineering are not the same thing. Programming Fundamentals showed me that difference clearly. When Dr Bilal started teaching Python, I felt something shift inside my thinking. For the first time, a subject was not simply asking me to repeat information back. It was asking me to build logic.

That was the reason I connected to PF so strongly. Python felt clean, structured, and honest. If a program was broken, I could trace the cause. If the logic was weak, the result exposed it. That kind of learning suited me much more than subjects that depended mainly on memorization.

What the late nights really meant

The PF labs became some of the most defining parts of my first semester. I spent many nights working on them in the hostel, sometimes not because I was stuck, but because I did not want to leave the work in a state that felt ordinary. Running code was not enough for me. I wanted to understand why it worked and whether it could be better.

That habit of staying with the problem until it felt right taught me more than a single lab could ever teach. It trained my patience, sharpened my standards, and showed me how naturally I connect with logic-based work.

What PF gave me beyond a grade

PF was not only one of my strongest subjects academically. It was the first course that made me feel I truly belong in this field. It gave me confidence early in CE, and that confidence mattered because first semester was not easy overall. I needed a subject that reminded me that I was not just surviving the degree. I was starting to become the kind of student the degree was shaping.
