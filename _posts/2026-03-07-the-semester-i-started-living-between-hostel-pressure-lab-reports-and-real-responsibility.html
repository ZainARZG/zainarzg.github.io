My first semester did not feel divided neatly into lectures, labs, and rest. Everything overlapped. The hostel routine, the pressure of submissions, the cost and availability of printing, and the expectation to stay ready all the time made daily life much heavier than the timetable looked on paper.

PF and AICT labs shaped a big part of that routine. I often stayed up late trying to make work cleaner, better organized, and worthy of submission. I also spent serious time on lab reports, and sometimes even helped groupmates with theirs. That part of the semester was rarely visible in the final result, but it consumed a lot of real energy.

One of the better memories from that pressure was my AICT presentation with Munnazza. I chose Seerat ul Nabi (PBUH) as the topic, prepared it carefully in PowerPoint, presented it with confidence, and received full marks. That moment reminded me that I do not only work well alone in front of a screen. I can also stand up and communicate my work clearly.

That entire period made me more responsible. It taught me that engineering student life includes many invisible tasks that never appear in the course title but still shape the person doing the degree.
