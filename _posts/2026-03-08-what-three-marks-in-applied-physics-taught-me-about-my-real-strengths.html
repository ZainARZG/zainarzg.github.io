--- layout: post title: "What Three Marks in Applied Physics Taught Me About My Real Strengths" date: 2025-03-08 00:00:00 +0500 summary: "A weak quiz result taught me more about how I learn than an easy A ever could." subpoints: - "My weak start in Applied Physics theory showed me that I struggle when a subject asks for ratta instead of understanding." - "Programming Fundamentals kept my confidence alive because it reminded me that practical work and logic are still my natural strengths." - "Recovering to an A- in Applied Physics proved that I can improve even in subjects that do not come naturally." ---

In Applied Physics theory, I had one of the most uncomfortable wake-up calls of my first semester. I performed poorly in the first quiz and got only three marks. That result was hard to accept, but it was also honest. It showed me that I cannot approach every subject in the same way.

I am naturally stronger in practical work, mathematics, and logic-based learning. That is one reason Programming Fundamentals felt so alive to me. Applied Physics theory was different. It demanded a style of preparation that I did not enjoy and had not yet adapted to.

Instead of pretending the subject was unfair, I tried to understand what the real issue was. My mids and second quiz were average, but I pushed harder before finals and changed my approach. In the end, I recovered to an A- in theory and an A in lab.

That result mattered to me because it proved that even when a subject is not naturally mine, I can still fight my way into a respectable outcome if I stay honest about the gap.
