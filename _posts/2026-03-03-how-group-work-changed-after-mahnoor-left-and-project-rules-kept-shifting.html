One part of university life that surprised me was how much the atmosphere of a class can change when one reliable person leaves. Mahnoor Fatima had taken on important CR and GR responsibility after earlier casual selections created problems. She had strong academic standing and handled things seriously.

When she transferred to Lahore, the effect was bigger than it looked from outside. Communication and timing started slipping more often. Delayed quizzes, assignments, and coordination issues made it clear that class routine depends heavily on a few people actually doing their part.

Around the same time, my own DBS project situation kept changing. I first wanted to work alone because it was the first time I genuinely wanted to test myself independently. I even turned down other group options. But later, when pair-or-single rules became more practical than personal preference, I ended up working with Muhammad Umair.

That period taught me something useful: engineering work is not only about technical skill. It is also about adapting to changing people, changing rules, and changing expectations without losing your direction.
